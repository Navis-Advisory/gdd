## Governing thought

Pass at ~6× ARR: the five-module record kills the growth thesis — market headroom refuted, pricing refuted as a switching-cost annuity, share gain without surviving support — while the real-but-narrow, decay-gated compliance moat cannot carry it; a revisit requires data-room asset redefinition and reprice, and the deal breakers gating that path do not clear outside-in.

## Key line

Ordered by what the IC will challenge first, following the client's own thesis structure (market → share gain → pricing → moat) and closing on what a revisit would take: sizing first (it contradicts the "under-penetrated" prior), then displacement, then monetization, then the moat itself, then the deal-structure consequence.

1. **The market lever fails.** CY2025 NA pest-control FSM spend sits in a $55–116M working span — below the ~$200M headroom bar in every defensible construction from either independent leg (max defensible high $167M) — and the underlying demand base grows at 1.4–6.0%/yr against the 14.9%/yr the thesis requires. Untouched by the five-module scope extension; still the sturdiest kill in the ledger. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]
2. **Share gain from generic FSM tools carries no surviving support, and the v2 record makes the contest harder, not easier.** The vertical's top end is consolidator-owned; the largest "generic," ServiceTitan, owns pest-compliance capability first-party and is now running the segment's AI feature race off a public-company R&D base with no funded AI-native challenger in sight; the one clean feature gap that survives (no horizontal ships comparable native compliance) establishes the gap, not the capture. The displacement-pool size and the switching-direction claim still carry no un-contested ledger support. [F6, F10, F11, F17]
3. **Pricing is refuted — what exists is a switching-cost annuity, not pricing power.** If teaser scale is real and in-segment, implied ARPU already sits at/above vertical-peer mid-market pricing; the company module confirms the underlying mechanism is data-lock and workflow-depth switching costs at the deep-compliance tier, not brand or price power — the one documented vertical repricing event (PestPac, Oct-2024) was contract-monetized and tied to a cross-corroborated churn-direction cluster, not a stayed-and-paid-more story; vertical pricing power is refuted outright on reachable rungs. [F13, F19, F25, F26]
4. **The compliance moat is real but narrow, and its durability is decay-gated — it cannot carry the thesis alone.** The moat is not a regulatory/licensing mechanism, and its federal demand driver has already been rescinded, leaving durability resting on state regimes that are no longer trending up with zero evidenced growth tailwind; horizontals fail the one-release-cycle catch-up test on cadence evidence; on buyer-language frequency, compliance is a clean win-driver but ranks #4, not top-3 — below the three battleground criteria (price/support/ease-of-use) where horizontals are at parity or advantaged. [F10, F14, F20, F23, F24, F27, F28]
5. **The asset being priced remains undefined, and the deal breakers that would gate a revisit do not clear outside-in.** Teaser ~$40M ARR (~90% NA ⇒ ~$36M) implies a 31–65% share of the $55–116M span — jointly consistent with a #2 rank and the observed vendor set only at simultaneous extremes — and the record's own closure cross-check refuses the joint headline claims. Of the three deal breakers the client named: the customer-concentration screen trips and is unchaseable outside-in; the in-segment-ARR screen cannot clear without an out-of-segment mix well above the observed comparable-class ceiling; the third breaker's own "not triggered" disposition is itself disputed (see Risks & sensitivities).
