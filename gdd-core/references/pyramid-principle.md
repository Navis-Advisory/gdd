# Pyramid principle (storyline construction)

Reference for `gdd-storyliner`.

## Governing thought

The answer to the client's decision question, one sentence, stated
first. Not a topic ("market attractiveness") — a claim ("the market
supports the thesis at the proposed entry multiple").

Patterns by decision type:
- **Invest**: "The thesis holds: [market condition], [position
  condition], [risk bound]."
- **Pass**: "The thesis fails on [the one condition that kills it];
  the rest is moot." (A pass storyline leads with the kill, not a
  tour of everything checked.)
- **Conditioned**: "Attractive at/if [explicit condition — price,
  structure, fix]; breaks above/without it." The condition must come
  from D6 flip points or the red team, not hedging instinct.
- **Can't-tell is not a governing thought.** If evidence genuinely
  can't answer (unresolvable disputes dominate), the governing thought
  is about the decision under uncertainty: "Current evidence cannot
  establish [X]; deciding now means underwriting [specific risk]."

## Key line

3–5 claims that together make the governing thought MECE. The standard
diligence frame — market / competitive position / customer proof /
risks-bounded — fits most deals; depart when the governing thought
demands it (a pass-storyline's key line is the kill chain).

Checks:
- Each claim is a claim, not a section title.
- Together they exhaust the governing thought (nothing load-bearing
  outside them); none overlaps another (D3 applies here too).
- Order by what the audience will challenge first, not by module
  order.

## Supports

Findings (by ledger id) beneath each key-line claim.
- **Vertical logic**: each level answers "why/how is the level above
  true?" — if a support doesn't help prove its claim, it's decoration;
  cut it.
- **Horizontal logic**: siblings are same-kind (all evidence, or all
  sub-arguments) and ordered (magnitude, time, or logic — pick one).
- Numbers verbatim from the ledger; the taxonomy rounding rule is the
  only transformation allowed.

## Trace map

Claim → finding ids → source ids, rendered as a table at the end of
STORYLINE.md. The audit trail is part of the deliverable: it is what
lets the client's IC challenge any sentence and land on a source in
two hops. A claim that can't be traced doesn't go in the storyline —
that constraint, applied honestly, is most of GDD's value.
